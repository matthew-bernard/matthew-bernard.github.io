Spectrum of the Top-to-Random Chain on Two-Coloured Words

A note based on discussions with Professor Steven N. Evans

Published

November 10, 2026

Abstract. Let \(\Omega_n\) be the set of words containing \(n\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(n\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\). At each step the leftmost letter is removed and reinserted, uniformly at random, into one of the \(2n\) slots determined by the remaining \(2n-1\) letters. We show that the transition matrix is diagonalizable, that its eigenvalues are \(1\) and \(j/(2n)\) for \(0\le j\le 2n-2\), and that \(j/(2n)\) has multiplicity equal to the number of words of length \(2n-2-j\) in \(\mathtt{R},\mathtt{G}\) with at most \(n-1\) copies of each letter. The proof lumps the chain from the top-to-random shuffle of \(2n\) distinct cards and determines the \(\mathfrak{S}_{2n}\)-module structure of the eigenspaces of that shuffle by an equivariant filtration in the spirit of Brown’s theory of left-regular bands. We work out the cases \(n\le 6\) in detail; give equivalent forms of the multiplicities, a Pascal-type recurrence and a generating function; prove that the multiplicities in the lower half of the spectrum have no hypergeometric closed form; split the spectrum along the Specht modules \(S^{(2n-i,i)}\); prove that the colour-symmetric and colour-antisymmetric parts have the same spectrum apart from the two largest eigenvalues; and examine a proposed binomial formula for the multiplicities.

The chain and the main result

The chain

Fix \(n\ge 1\) and put \(N=2n\). Let \(\Omega_n\) be the set of words of length \(N\) in the letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(\mathtt{G}\) that contain \(n\) of each, so \(\lvert \Omega_n\rvert=\binom{2n}{n}\). One step of the chain removes the leftmost letter of the current word. The remaining \(N-1\) letters determine \(N\) slots (before the first letter, between consecutive letters, and after the last), and the removed letter is inserted into one of them, chosen uniformly at random. Thus

\[ P(w,w')=\frac1N\,\#\{\text{slots whose use turns $w$ into $w'$}\}. \]

For example, from \(\mathtt{RGRG}\) we remove \(\mathtt{R}\) and are left with \(\mathtt{GRG}\); the four insertions give \(\mathtt{RGRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRGR}\), so \(P(\mathtt{RGRG},\cdot\,)\) puts mass \(\tfrac14,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\) on \(\mathtt{RGRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRGR}\).

The matrix \(P\) acts on functions \(f\colon\Omega_n\to\mathbb{C}\) by \((Pf)(w)=\sum_{w'}P(w,w')f(w')\). Inserting the removed letter into the \(p\)-th slot moves the first letter to position \(p\). Regarding a word as a map \(w\colon[N]\to\{\mathtt{R},\mathtt{G}\}\) from positions to letters, this says

\[ (Pf)(w)=\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^{N}f(w\circ\pi_p),\qquad \pi_p(q)=\begin{cases}q+1,&q<p,\\ 1,&q=p,\\ q,&q>p,\end{cases} \tag{1}\]

with \(\pi_p\in\mathfrak{S}_N\) a permutation of positions. We use this description in Section 5 and Section 7.

Conventions

Throughout, \(j\) is the numerator of an eigenvalue \(j/N\) and

\[ L=N-2-j . \]

Binomial coefficients \(\binom{L}{i}\) with \(L\ge0\) vanish unless \(0\le i\le L\). We write \(B(L,k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{L}{i}\) for partial row sums of Pascal’s triangle, \(d_m\) for the number of derangements of \(m\) objects, and \(C_n=\binom{2n}{n}/(n+1)\) for the Catalan numbers.

The main result

Theorem 1 The matrix \(P\) is diagonalizable. Its eigenvalues are \(1\), with multiplicity \(1\), and \(j/N\) for \(0\le j\le N-2\), with multiplicity

\[ m_n(j)=\sum_{i=\max(0,\,L-n+1)}^{\min(L,\,n-1)}\binom{L}{i},\qquad L=N-2-j, \tag{2}\]

which is the number of words of length \(L\) in \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(\mathtt{G}\) that contain at most \(n-1\) copies of each letter. In particular \((N-1)/N\) is not an eigenvalue. Setting \(m_n(N-1)=0\) and \(m_n(N)=1\), we have for every \(0\le j\le N\)

\[ m_n(j)=\sum_{\substack{r+g=j\\ 0\le r,\,g\le n}} D(n-r,\,n-g),\qquad D(a,b)=\begin{cases}\binom{a+b-2}{a-1},& a,b\ge 1,\\ 1,& a=b=0,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \tag{3}\]

The multiplicities for \(n\le 6\) are listed in Table 1; they agree with the spectra computed directly from the matrices (Section 3 and Section 9).

Remark. Distinct cards. For the top-to-random shuffle of \(N\) distinct cards the eigenvalues are again \(j/N\), \(j\in\{0,\dots,N-2,N\}\), and \(j/N\) has multiplicity \(\binom{N}{j}d_{N-j}\), the number of permutations of \([N]\) with exactly \(j\) fixed points (Bidigare et al. 1999; Brown and Diaconis 1998; Brown 2000; Diaconis et al. 1992). The value \((N-1)/N\) is missing for the same reason as here: \(d_1=0\). Equation 3 is the coloured analogue of \(\sum_{\lvert X\rvert=j}d_{N-j}\): subsets \(X\) of the cards are replaced by sub-multisets with \(r\) red and \(g\) green cards, and the derangement number \(d_{N-j}\) of the remaining cards is replaced by the coloured derangement number \(D(a,b)\) of the remaining \(a\) red and \(b\) green cards. For \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\), \(D(a,b)\) is the number of words with \(a\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(b\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\) that begin with \(\mathtt{R}\) and end with \(\mathtt{G}\).

Remark. Plan. Section 2 makes the colour-swap symmetry precise, and Section 3 works out \(n=1,2,3,5\). Section 4 gives equivalent forms of Equation 2. Section 5 proves Theorem 1 in three steps: the chain is a lumping of the distinct-card shuffle (Proposition 3); the \(\mathfrak{S}_N\)-module structure of the eigenspaces of the distinct-card shuffle is determined by an equivariant filtration (Theorem 2); and Frobenius reciprocity counts the invariants. The same argument handles decks of arbitrary content (Corollary 1). Section 6 discusses how far Equation 2 simplifies, including a proof that the lower half of the spectrum has no hypergeometric closed form (Theorem 3). Section 7 splits the spectrum along Specht modules and proves the colour-swap theorem (Theorem 4). Section 8 examines a proposed binomial formula, and Section 9 lists the computer checks; the code is in Appendix A.

The colour-swap symmetry

Let \(\sigma\colon\Omega_n\to\Omega_n\) exchange \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(\mathtt{G}\) in every position, so for instance \(\sigma(\mathtt{RRGRGG})=\mathtt{GGRGRR}\).

  • \(\sigma\) is an involution without fixed points (it changes the first letter), so \(\Omega_n\) splits into \(\binom{2n}{n}/2\) pairs \(\{x,\sigma x\}\).
  • The chain does not see the names of the colours: \(P(\sigma x,\sigma y)=P(x,y)\).

Let \((Sf)(x)=f(\sigma x)\). Then \(S^2=I\) and \(SP=PS\). Put

\[ V_+=\{f:\ f(\sigma x)=f(x)\ \text{for all }x\},\qquad V_-=\{f:\ f(\sigma x)=-f(x)\ \text{for all }x\}. \]

Proposition 1 The two parts have the following properties.

(i) \(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega_n}=V_+\oplus V_-\), via \(f=\tfrac12(f+Sf)+\tfrac12(f-Sf)\).

(ii) \(V_+\) and \(V_-\) are \(P\)-invariant.

(iii) Choose a representative \(x\) in each pair \(O=\{x,\sigma x\}\). The functions \(\mathbf 1_x+\mathbf 1_{\sigma x}\) form a basis of \(V_+\) and the functions \(\mathbf 1_x-\mathbf 1_{\sigma x}\) a basis of \(V_-\); in particular \(\dim V_\pm=\binom{2n}{n}/2\).

(iv) In these bases \(P\) acts on \(V_\pm\) by the matrices

\[ P_\pm(O,O')=P(x,y)\pm P(x,\sigma y), \]

where \(x\) and \(y\) are the chosen representatives of \(O\) and \(O'\). Hence the spectrum of \(P\) is the union, with multiplicities, of the spectra of \(P_+\) and \(P_-\).

Proof. (i) and (ii) follow from \(S^2=I\) and \(SP=PS\), and (iii) is immediate. For (iv), if \(f=\sum_{O'}a_{O'}(\mathbf 1_y\pm\mathbf 1_{\sigma y})\) then \((Pf)(x)=\sum_{O'}a_{O'}\bigl(P(x,y)\pm P(x,\sigma y)\bigr)\), and \((Pf)(x)\) is the coefficient of \(\mathbf 1_x\pm\mathbf 1_{\sigma x}\) in \(Pf\).

The matrix \(P_+\) is the transition matrix of the lumped chain on the pairs \(\{x,\sigma x\}\), that is, of the chain observed only up to colour swap (for instance, by recording at each position whether its letter agrees with the first letter); it is a genuine Markov chain because \(P\) commutes with \(\sigma\). The matrix \(P_-\) is the signed version and is not stochastic. The constant function lies in \(V_+\), so the eigenvalue \(1\) belongs to \(P_+\). Theorem 4 below shows that \(P_+\) and \(P_-\) have the same spectrum, except that \(1\) belongs to \(P_+\) only and \((N-2)/N\) to \(P_-\) only.

Small cases

\(n=1\)

Here \(\Omega_1=\{\mathtt{RG},\mathtt{GR}\}\). From either state, removing the first letter leaves a single letter, and the two insertions give \(\mathtt{RG}\) and \(\mathtt{GR}\). So \(P=\frac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), with eigenvalue \(1\) (constant eigenvector, in \(V_+\)) and \(0\) (eigenvector \((1,-1)\), in \(V_-\)). The value \((N-1)/N=\tfrac12\) does not occur.

\(n=2\)

Number the six states

\[ 1=\mathtt{RRGG},\quad 2=\mathtt{RGRG},\quad 3=\mathtt{RGGR},\quad 4=\mathtt{GRRG},\quad 5=\mathtt{GRGR},\quad 6=\mathtt{GGRR}. \]

Removing the first letter and reinserting it into each of the four slots in turn gives

\[ 4P=\begin{pmatrix} 2&1&1&0&0&0\\ 0&1&0&2&1&0\\ 0&0&1&0&1&2\\ 2&1&0&1&0&0\\ 0&1&2&0&1&0\\ 0&0&0&1&1&2 \end{pmatrix}. \]

For instance, from \(\mathtt{RGRG}\) we are left with \(\mathtt{GRG}\), and the insertions give \(\mathtt{RGRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRRG}\), \(\mathtt{GRGR}\), which is the second row.

The colour swap pairs \(1\leftrightarrow 6\), \(2\leftrightarrow 5\), \(3\leftrightarrow 4\).

Symmetric part. Functions in \(V_+\) have the form \(f=(a,b,c,c,b,a)\), and \(4P\) sends \((a,b,c)\) to \((2a+b+c,\ 2b+2c,\ 2a+b+c)\). So

\[ 4P_+=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\0&2&2\\2&1&1\end{pmatrix}. \]

Its trace is \(5\); its rows sum to \(4\), so \(4\) is an eigenvalue; its first and third rows are equal, so \(0\) is an eigenvalue; and the trace forces the third eigenvalue to be \(1\). Thus \(P_+\) has eigenvalues \(1,\tfrac14,0\).

Antisymmetric part. Functions in \(V_-\) have the form \(f=(a,b,c,-c,-b,-a)\), and \(4P\) sends \((a,b,c)\) to \((2a+b+c,\ -2c,\ -2a-b+c)\). So

\[ 4P_-=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\0&0&-2\\-2&-1&1\end{pmatrix}, \]

with characteristic polynomial \(x(x-1)(x-2)\). Thus \(P_-\) has eigenvalues \(\tfrac12,\tfrac14,0\).

The spectrum of \(P\) is therefore \(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac14,0,0\), as Theorem 1 predicts: for \(j=0,1,2\) we have \(L=2,1,0\), and the words of length \(L\) with at most one copy of each letter are \(\mathtt{RG}\) and \(\mathtt{GR}\); \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(\mathtt{G}\); and the empty word. The value \(\tfrac34\) does not occur. Right eigenvectors (functions on the states, listed in the order above) are given in Table 2.

\(n=3\)

There are \(\binom63=20\) states. Each row of \(6P\) is obtained by removing the first letter and reinserting it into each of the \(6\) slots; three sample rows are

from to (entry of \(6P\))
\(\mathtt{RRRGGG}\) \(\mathtt{RRRGGG}\) (3), \(\mathtt{RRGGGR}\) (1), \(\mathtt{RRGGRG}\) (1), \(\mathtt{RRGRGG}\) (1)
\(\mathtt{RGRGRG}\) \(\mathtt{GRGRGR}\) (1), \(\mathtt{GRGRRG}\) (2), \(\mathtt{GRRGRG}\) (2), \(\mathtt{RGRGRG}\) (1)
\(\mathtt{RRGGGR}\) \(\mathtt{RGGGRR}\) (2), \(\mathtt{RGGRGR}\) (1), \(\mathtt{RGRGGR}\) (1), \(\mathtt{RRGGGR}\) (2)

The eigenvalues of \(6P\) are integers. Computing \(\dim\ker(6P-jI)\) exactly, by integer row reduction, for each \(j\) gives the multiplicities below; they add up to \(20\), so \(P\) is diagonalizable with spectrum

\[ 1,\quad \tfrac23,\quad \tfrac12\ (\times2),\quad \tfrac13\ (\times4),\quad \tfrac16\ (\times6),\quad 0\ (\times6), \]

accounting for all \(20\) states. Table 3 compares this with Theorem 1.

The colour swap splits the \(20\)-dimensional space into two \(10\)-dimensional parts, and the eigenvalues of \(6P_\pm\) are

\[ 6P_+:\ 6,3,2,2,1,1,1,0,0,0,\qquad 6P_-:\ 4,3,2,2,1,1,1,0,0,0 . \]

The two lists agree except at the top: the symmetric part contains the stationary eigenvalue \(1\) and the antisymmetric part contains \(\tfrac23\), the second largest eigenvalue. The same happens for \(n=2\), where the parts are \(\{1,\tfrac14,0\}\) and \(\{\tfrac12,\tfrac14,0\}\); Theorem 4 proves this pattern for all \(n\).

\(n=5\)

There are \(252\) states. Table 4 lists the multiplicities, both as predicted by Equation 2 and as found from the matrix.

The computed column was obtained in four independent ways.

(i) Floating-point eigenvalues. The \(252\) eigenvalues of \(P\) were computed with a standard dense eigensolver. Every value of \(10\lambda\) lies within \(3.2\times10^{-13}\) of an integer, every imaginary part is below \(2.2\times10^{-14}\), and counting the eigenvalues at each integer gives the formula column.

(ii) Exact eigenspace dimensions. Since \(10P\) has integer entries, the dimensions of the kernels of \(10P-jI\) and of \((10P-jI)^2\) can be computed exactly, by integer row reduction. For every \(j\) the two numbers coincide and equal \(m_5(j)\), and they add up to \(252\). Hence \(10P\) is diagonalizable, its only eigenvalues are \(0,1,\dots,8\) and \(10\), and the multiplicities are those of Table 4. This proves Theorem 1 for \(n=5\) independently of Section 5.

(iii) Singular values. For each \(j\), the singular values of \(10P-jI\) separate cleanly: those counted as zero are at most \(4.1\times10^{-15}\), and the next smallest is at least \(0.139\). The resulting kernel dimensions agree with (ii).

(iv) Traces of powers. In exact integer arithmetic, \(\operatorname{tr}\bigl((10P)^k\bigr)=\sum_j m_5(j)\,j^k\) for every \(0\le k\le 18\). For example the common values are \(420\), \(1402\), \(247830\), \(11977281982\) and \(1021709094257451262\) for \(k=1,2,5,10,18\).

Equivalent forms of the multiplicities

Proposition 2 Let \(0\le j\le N-2\) and \(L=N-2-j\). Each of the following equals \(m_n(j)\).

(a) The number of words of length \(L\) in \(\mathtt{R},\mathtt{G}\) with at most \(n-1\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and at most \(n-1\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\).

(b) The number of words of length \(L\) in which the numbers of \(\mathtt{R}\)’s and \(\mathtt{G}\)’s differ by at most \(j\):

\[ m_n(j)=\sum_{\lvert 2i-L\rvert\le j}\binom{L}{i}=2^L\,\Pr\bigl(\lvert S_L\rvert\le j\bigr), \]

where \(S_L\) is the position of a simple \(\pm1\) random walk after \(L\) steps.

(c) \(2B(L,n-1)-2^L=2\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\binom Li-2^L\).

(d) \(S(L)\), where \(S(0)=1\) and \(S(\ell)=2S(\ell-1)-2\binom{\ell-1}{n-1}\) for \(1\le\ell\le L\).

(e) \(\sum_{r+g=j}D(n-r,n-g)\), with \(0\le r,g\le n\) and \(D\) as in Equation 3. This also holds for \(j=N-1\) and \(j=N\), with values \(0\) and \(1\).

Proof. (a) A word with \(i\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) has \(L-i\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\), and the conditions \(i\le n-1\) and \(L-i\le n-1\) say \(L-n+1\le i\le n-1\), which is the range of summation in Equation 2.

(b) The two conditions say \(2i-L\le 2n-2-L=j\) and \(L-2i\le j\).

(c) By the symmetry \(\binom Li=\binom L{L-i}\), \(\sum_{i\le L-n}\binom Li=\sum_{i\ge n}\binom Li=2^L-B(L,n-1)\), so the sum over \(L-n+1\le i\le n-1\) equals \(B(L,n-1)-\bigl(2^L-B(L,n-1)\bigr)\).

(d) Let \(S(\ell)\) be the number of words of length \(\ell\) with at most \(n-1\) copies of each letter. Extending each such word of length \(\ell-1\) by one letter gives \(2S(\ell-1)\) words of length \(\ell\). An extension is invalid exactly when it adds an \(n\)-th copy of a letter, and for each letter there are \(\binom{\ell-1}{n-1}\) such extensions (the other letter then occurs \(\ell-n\le n-1\) times, since \(\ell\le L\le 2n-2\)).

(e) Let \(j\le N-2\). A term with \(r=n\) or \(g=n\) vanishes, because \(D(0,b)=D(a,0)=0\) for \(a,b\ge1\) and \(r=g=n\) would force \(j=N\). For \(r,g\le n-1\) we have \(D(n-r,n-g)=\binom{2n-2-j}{n-r-1}=\binom{L}{n-1-r}\). As \(r\) runs over \(\max(0,j-n+1)\le r\le\min(j,n-1)\), the index \(i=n-1-r\) runs over \(\max(0,n-1-j)\le i\le\min(n-1,L)\), and \(n-1-j=L-n+1\), so this is the range in Equation 2. For \(j=N\) only \((r,g)=(n,n)\) occurs, giving \(D(0,0)=1\); for \(j=N-1\) the terms are \(D(0,1)=D(1,0)=0\).

When \(L\le n-1\), that is \(j\ge n-1\), no word of length \(L\) contains \(n\) copies of a letter, so \(m_n(j)=2^L\); this and other simplifications are discussed in Section 6.

Proof of the main theorem

Symmetric functions

We use the ring \(\Lambda\) of symmetric functions with the complete, elementary, power sum and Schur functions \(h_k\), \(e_k\), \(p_1=h_1=e_1\) and \(s_\lambda\), and the convention \(h_k=e_k=0\) for \(k<0\). Put \(H(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}h_kt^k\) and \(E(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}e_kt^k\), so that \(H(t)E(-t)=1\). The Frobenius characteristic \(\operatorname{ch}\) sends a representation of \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) to \(\Lambda^N\); it satisfies \(\operatorname{ch}\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_\mu}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}\mathbf 1=h_\mu\), \(\operatorname{ch}(\mathrm{sgn}_N)=e_N\), \(\operatorname{ch} S^\lambda=s_\lambda\) and \(\operatorname{ch}\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_a\times\mathfrak{S}_b}^{\mathfrak{S}_{a+b}}(V\otimes W)=\operatorname{ch} V\cdot\operatorname{ch} W\), and \(\langle \operatorname{ch} V,\,\operatorname{ch} W\rangle=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak{S}_N}(V,W)\) for the Hall inner product (Macdonald 1995; Sagan 2001).

For an operator \(A\) on a finite-dimensional space \(V\) we write \(V_\lambda(A)=\ker(A-\lambda)^{\dim V}\) for the generalized eigenspace. If a group acts on \(V\) commuting with \(A\), each \(V_\lambda(A)\) is a submodule, because the projection onto \(V_\lambda(A)\) along the other generalized eigenspaces is a polynomial in \(A\). For the same reason, a short exact sequence \(0\to V'\to V\to V''\to0\) of modules with compatible operators gives short exact sequences \(0\to V'_\lambda\to V_\lambda\to V''_\lambda\to0\), so characters of generalized eigenspaces add along filtrations.

Step 1: lumping

An arrangement of \(N\) distinct cards is a bijection \(c\colon[N]\to[N]\) from positions to cards; \(c(1)\) is the top card. The group \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) acts on arrangements by relabelling the cards, \(c\mapsto g\circ c\), and on functions by \((g\cdot f)(c)=f(g^{-1}\circ c)\); as a module, \(\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}\) is the regular representation, with characteristic \(h_1^N\). The top-to-random operator is

\[ (Tf)(c)=\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^N f(c\circ\pi_p), \]

where \(c\circ\pi_p\) is the arrangement obtained from \(c\) by moving the top card to position \(p\), with \(\pi_p\) as in Equation 1. Relabelling acts on the left and moves act on the right, so \(T\) commutes with relabelling. The random-to-top operator is \((Rf)(c)=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N f(i\cdot c)\), where \(i\cdot c\) is obtained from \(c\) by moving card \(i\) to the top. Moving the top card to position \(p\) is undone by moving the same card back to the top, so \(R(c,c')=T(c',c)\), that is \(R=T^{\mathsf T}\); \(R\) also commutes with relabelling.

Colour cards \(1,\dots,n\) red and \(n+1,\dots,N\) green, by \(\kappa\colon[N]\to\{\mathtt{R},\mathtt{G}\}\), and put \(\varphi(c)=\kappa\circ c\in\Omega_n\), the colour word of \(c\). Let \(\Gamma=\{g\in\mathfrak{S}_N:\kappa\circ g=\kappa\}=\mathfrak{S}_n\times\mathfrak{S}_n\). The fibres of \(\varphi\) are exactly the \(\Gamma\)-orbits.

Proposition 3 For every \(f\colon\Omega_n\to\mathbb{C}\) we have \(T(f\circ\varphi)=(Pf)\circ\varphi\). The map \(f\mapsto f\circ\varphi\) identifies \(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega_n}\) with the space \((\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N})^\Gamma\) of \(\Gamma\)-invariant functions and intertwines \(P\) with \(T\). Consequently, for every \(\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\) the algebraic multiplicity of \(\lambda\) as an eigenvalue of \(P\) equals \(\dim\bigl(V_\lambda(T)\bigr)^\Gamma\), where \(V=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}\).

Proof. \((f\circ\varphi)(c\circ\pi_p)=f(\kappa\circ c\circ\pi_p)=f(\varphi(c)\circ\pi_p)\); averaging over \(p\) and using Equation 1 gives \((Pf)(\varphi(c))\). A function on arrangements is \(\Gamma\)-invariant exactly when it is constant on the fibres of \(\varphi\), that is, of the form \(f\circ\varphi\). Finally, the projection onto \(V_\lambda(T)\) is a polynomial in \(T\), hence commutes with averaging over \(\Gamma\), so the generalized \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(T\) restricted to \(V^\Gamma\) is \(V_\lambda(T)\cap V^\Gamma=V_\lambda(T)^\Gamma\).

Injective words and the prefix filtration

Definition 1 An injective word of length \(k\) over \([N]\) is a sequence \(u=u_1u_2\cdots u_k\) of distinct elements of \([N]\); equivalently, an injective map \([k]\to[N]\), \(i\mapsto u_i\). Over the alphabet \([4]\), for example, \(3\,1\,4\) is an injective word of length \(3\) and \(3\,1\,3\) is not. There are \(N!/(N-k)!\) injective words of length \(k\); the empty word is the injective word of length \(0\), and the injective words of length \(N\) are the arrangements. (In other terminology, injective words of length \(k\) are \(k\)-permutations or partial permutations.)

Remark. With the product “concatenate, then delete every letter that has already appeared”, for instance \((3\,1)\cdot(1\,4\,2)=3\,1\,4\,2\), injective words form the free left-regular band. Its random walk on the chambers (the arrangements) driven by the one-letter words is random-to-top; this is how the Tsetlin library enters Brown’s theory (Brown 2000).

Let \(F_k\) be the space of functions on injective words of length \(k\), with \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) acting by relabelling. The stabilizer of a word is the group of permutations fixing each of its \(k\) letters, so

\[ F_k\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_1^k\times\mathfrak{S}_{N-k}}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}\mathbf 1,\qquad \operatorname{ch} F_k=h_1^k\,h_{N-k}. \]

Let \(U_k\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}\) be the space of functions \(f\) such that \(f(c)\) depends only on the top \(k\) cards \((c(1),\dots,c(k))\). The top \(k\) cards form an injective word of length \(k\), and every such word occurs, so \(U_k\cong F_k\) via \(f(c)=\tilde f(c(1)\cdots c(k))\). This gives a filtration by submodules

\[ U_0\subset U_1\subset\cdots\subset U_{N-1}=U_N=\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}, \]

where \(U_0\) is the space of constants and \(U_{N-1}=U_N\) because the top \(N-1\) cards determine the arrangement.

Lemma 1 Each \(U_k\) is invariant under \(R\). For \(1\le k\le N\) and \(f\in U_k\),

\[ NRf\equiv A_kf\pmod{U_{k-1}},\qquad (A_k\tilde f)(u)=\sum_{t=1}^{k-1}\tilde f\bigl(u_t\,u_1\cdots\widehat{u_t}\cdots u_k\bigr), \]

that is, \(A_k\) moves one of the first \(k-1\) letters to the front and leaves the last letter \(u_k\) in place (the hat denotes omission; \(A_1=0\)). The operator \(A_k\) preserves the functions on \(k\)-words that depend only on the first \(k-1\) letters, and acts on them as unnormalized random-to-top on \((k-1)\)-words.

Proof. Let \(u=c(1)\cdots c(k)\). If \(i=u_t\) with \(t\le k\), the top \(k\) cards of \(i\cdot c\) are \(u_t\,u_1\cdots\widehat{u_t}\cdots u_k\); if \(i\notin\{u_1,\dots,u_k\}\), they are \(i\,u_1\cdots u_{k-1}\). Hence

\[ N(Rf)(c)=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\tilde f\bigl(u_t\,u_1\cdots\widehat{u_t}\cdots u_k\bigr) +\sum_{i\notin\{u_1,\dots,u_k\}}\tilde f(i\,u_1\cdots u_{k-1}), \tag{4}\]

which depends only on \(u\); so \(Rf\in U_k\). Write the second sum as

\[ \sum_{i\notin\{u_1,\dots,u_{k-1}\}}\tilde f(i\,u_1\cdots u_{k-1}) \;-\;\tilde f(u_k\,u_1\cdots u_{k-1}). \]

The first term depends only on \(u_1\cdots u_{k-1}\) and so lies in \(U_{k-1}\); the subtracted term cancels the term \(t=k\) of the first sum in Equation 4. This proves the congruence. If \(\tilde f(u)\) depends only on \(u_1\cdots u_{k-1}\), then moving \(u_t\) (\(t\le k-1\)) to the front rearranges the first \(k-1\) letters, so \(A_k\tilde f\) again depends only on them, through unnormalized random-to-top on \((k-1)\)-words.

Step 2: the eigenspaces of the distinct-card shuffle

Theorem 2 For \(N\ge0\) and \(\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\) let \(c_N(\ell)\in\Lambda^N\) be the Frobenius characteristic of the generalized eigenspace of \(NR\) on \(\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N}\) for the eigenvalue \(\ell\), with \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) acting by relabelling. Then

\[ c_N(\ell)=h_\ell\,\delta_{N-\ell}\quad(0\le\ell\le N),\qquad c_N(\ell)=0\ \text{otherwise}, \]

where

\[ \sum_{m\ge0}\delta_mt^m=\frac{E(-t)}{1-h_1t},\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad \delta_m=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^i e_i\,h_1^{m-i}. \]

The same holds for \(NT\). In particular \(NR\) and \(NT\) have only the eigenvalues \(0,1,\dots,N-2\) and \(N\), and the eigenvalue \(\ell\) has multiplicity \(\binom N\ell d_{N-\ell}\).

Proof. The pieces. For \(1\le k\le N\) let \(Q_k=U_k/U_{k-1}\). By Lemma 1, \(NR\) induces on \(Q_k\) the same operator that \(A_k\) induces on \(F_k/F_{k-1}\), where \(F_{k-1}\) is embedded in \(F_k\) as the functions of the first \(k-1\) letters. So the \(\ell\)-part of \(Q_k\) has characteristic equal to that of \((F_k,A_k)\) minus that of \((F_{k-1},A_k|_{F_{k-1}})\).

The space \(F_k\) is the direct sum, over \(k\)-subsets \(X\subseteq[N]\) and \(s\in X\), of the functions on injective words with letter set \(X\) and last letter \(s\). The operator \(A_k\) preserves each summand and acts on it as unnormalized random-to-top on the orderings of \(X\setminus\{s\}\). Relabelling permutes the summands transitively, with stabilizer \(\mathfrak{S}_{X\setminus\{s\}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\{s\}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{[N]\setminus X}\cong \mathfrak{S}_{k-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_1\times\mathfrak{S}_{N-k}\). Hence \((F_k,A_k)\) is induced from \((\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_{k-1}},(k-1)R)\otimes\mathbf 1\otimes\mathbf 1\), and its \(\ell\)-part has characteristic \(c_{k-1}(\ell)\,h_1h_{N-k}\). In the same way, \((F_{k-1},A_k|_{F_{k-1}})\) is induced from \(\mathfrak{S}_{k-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{N-k+1}\), and its \(\ell\)-part has characteristic \(c_{k-1}(\ell)\,h_{N-k+1}\).

The recursion. On \(U_0\), the constants, \(NR\) acts as \(N\). Adding up the pieces of the filtration gives, for all \(N\ge0\),

\[ c_N(\ell)=[\ell=N]\,h_N+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bigl(h_1h_{N-k}-h_{N-k+1}\bigr)\,c_{k-1}(\ell), \tag{5}\]

so in particular \(c_0(\ell)=[\ell=0]\). (For \(k=N\) the coefficient vanishes, matching \(U_N=U_{N-1}\).)

Solving. Let \(C_\ell(t)=\sum_{N\ge0}c_N(\ell)t^N\). Multiplying Equation 5 by \(t^N\) and summing,

\[ C_\ell=h_\ell t^\ell+t\Bigl(h_1H(t)-\frac{H(t)-1}{t}\Bigr)C_\ell =h_\ell t^\ell+\bigl(1-H(t)+t\,h_1H(t)\bigr)C_\ell, \]

so \(C_\ell\,H(t)(1-h_1t)=h_\ell t^\ell\) and \(C_\ell=h_\ell t^\ell E(-t)/(1-h_1t)=h_\ell t^\ell\sum_m\delta_mt^m\). Reading off the coefficient of \(t^N\) gives the formula for \(c_N(\ell)\).

Dimensions. A representation with characteristic \(F\in\Lambda^m\) has dimension \(\langle F,\,h_1^m\rangle\); since \(\dim\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_i\times\mathfrak{S}_1^{m-i}}(\mathrm{sgn}\otimes\mathbf 1)=m!/i!\), we get \(\dim\delta_m=\sum_i(-1)^im!/i!=d_m\) and \(\dim h_\ell\delta_{N-\ell}=\binom N\ell d_{N-\ell}\). Also \(\delta_1=h_1-e_1=0\).

The operator \(T\). \(T=R^{\mathsf T}\) is the adjoint of \(R\) for the standard inner product, which is invariant under relabelling. Hence \(V_\ell(NT)\) is the orthogonal complement of \(\bigoplus_{\ell'\ne\ell}V_{\ell'}(NR)\), which is isomorphic as a module to the dual of \(V_\ell(NR)\). Characters of \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) are real, so the dual has the same characteristic.

Remark. Relation to Brown’s theory. Brown’s theorem for random walks on left-regular bands (Brown 2000) (and, for hyperplane arrangements, (Bidigare et al. 1999; Brown and Diaconis 1998)) indexes the eigenvalues by the support lattice, here the Boolean lattice of subsets \(X\) of the cards with \(\lambda_X=\lvert X\rvert/N\), and gives the multiplicities \(m_X=\sum_{Y\supseteq X}\mu(X,Y)\,(N-\lvert Y\rvert)!=d_{N-\lvert X\rvert}\) through the Möbius function \(\mu(X,Y)=(-1)^{\lvert Y\setminus X\rvert}\). Theorem 2 is the \(\mathfrak{S}_N\)-equivariant version: the factor \(1/H(t)=E(-t)\) is the Möbius function with the symmetric group acting, which is why it appears through the sign characters \(e_i\). We used the prefix filtration instead of Brown’s idempotent construction because it is visibly \(\mathfrak{S}_N\)-stable; Saliola (Saliola 2012) gives explicit eigenvectors, which would be the starting point for an idempotent-based equivariant version. The function \(\delta_m\) is the characteristic of the derangement representation of \(\mathfrak{S}_m\) (Désarménien and Wachs 1993; Reiner and Webb 2004), and the module structure in Theorem 2 is, we believe, also recorded in (Uyemura-Reyes 2002; Reiner et al. 2014); we have not checked the precise statements there.

Lemma 2 Let \(V\) be a representation of \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) and \(F,F'\in\Lambda\).

(c) holds because \(\{h_\lambda\}\) and \(\{m_\lambda\}\) are dual bases.

Proof. We prove Theorem 1. By Proposition 3 and Theorem 2, the eigenvalues of \(P\) lie in \(\{j/N:0\le j\le N-2\}\cup\{1\}\), and by Lemma 2 the algebraic multiplicity of \(j/N\) is

Only two variables are needed. In two variables \(e_1=x+y\), \(e_2=xy\) and \(e_i=0\) for \(i\ge3\), so

Then \(T=R^{\mathsf T}\) is diagonalizable, and so is its restriction \(P\) to the invariant subspace \((\mathbb{C}^{\mathfrak{S}_N})^\Gamma\) (Proposition 3).

Remark. The factor \(xy\) in \(\delta_m(x,y)=xy(x+y)^{m-2}\) is why \(D(a,b)\) counts the words that begin with \(\mathtt{R}\) and end with \(\mathtt{G}\) (see the remark on distinct cards in Section 1), and \(\delta_1=0\) is why \((N-1)/N\) never occurs: a single card cannot be deranged.

Proposition 4 Let \(0\le j\le N-2\) and \(L=N-2-j\).

(i) If \(j\ge n-1\) (equivalently \(L\le n-1\)), then \(m_n(j)=2^L=2^{2n-2-j}\).

(ii) If \(j\le n-2\), then \(m_n(j)=2^L-2B(L,n-2-j)\): all words of length \(L\) minus two tails of \(n-1-j\) binomial coefficients each.

(iii) For all \(j\), \(m_n(j)\ge 2^L\bigl(1-2e^{-j^2/(4n)}\bigr)\).

Proof. (i) A word of length \(L\le n-1\) cannot contain \(n\) copies of a letter. (ii) By the symmetry of binomial coefficients, \(2B(L,n-1)-2^L=2^L-2\sum_{i\ge n}\binom Li=2^L-2\sum_{i\le L-n}\binom Li\), and \(L-n=n-2-j\). (iii) If \(L=0\) then \(m_n(j)=1\) and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, by Proposition 2(b) and Hoeffding’s inequality (Hoeffding 1963), \(1-2^{-L}m_n(j)=\Pr(\lvert S_L\rvert\ge j+1)\le 2e^{-(j+1)^2/(2L)}\le2e^{-j^2/(4n)}\), since \(L<2n\).

Pascal’s rule

Proposition 5 For \(n\ge1\) and \(0\le L\le 2n-2\) put \(T(n,L)=m_n(2n-2-L)\), the multiplicity at depth \(L\) below the top eigenvalue \((N-2)/N\), and put \(T(n,L)=0\) for \(L>2n-2\). Then \(T(n,0)=1\), and for \(n\ge2\) and \(1\le L\le 2n-2\),

\[ T(n,L)=T(n,L-1)+T(n-1,L-1). \]

Equivalently, \(m_n(j)=m_n(j+1)+m_{n-1}(j-1)\) for \(1\le j\le 2n-3\), and \(m_n(0)=m_n(1)\).

Proof. \(T(n,L)\) counts the words of length \(L\) with at most \(n-1\) copies of each letter. Deleting the last letter of such a word gives such a word of length \(L-1\). Conversely, a valid word \(u\) of length \(L-1\le 2n-3\) cannot contain \(n-1\) copies of both letters, so it can be extended by at least one letter; it can be extended by both letters exactly when it contains at most \(n-2\) copies of each, and these words are counted by \(T(n-1,L-1)\), which is \(0\) when \(L-1=2n-3\). Hence \(T(n,L)=T(n,L-1)+T(n-1,L-1)\).

A generating function

Proposition 6 Let \(C(t)=\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-4t}\bigr)/(2t)=\sum_{k\ge0}C_kt^k\) be the Catalan generating function. Then

\[ \sum_{n\ge1}t^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2n-2}m_n(j)\,s^j =\frac{1+s\,tC(t)}{\bigl(1-s\,tC(t)\bigr)\bigl(1-s^2t\bigr)\sqrt{1-4t}} . \]

Adding the contribution of the eigenvalue \(1\),

\[ \sum_{n\ge1}s^{2n}t^{n-1}=\frac{s^2}{1-s^2t}, \]

gives the generating function \(\sum_{n\ge1}t^{n-1}\sum_\lambda s^{N\lambda}\) of the complete spectra, the inner sum running over the eigenvalues with multiplicity. At \(s=0\) the right-hand side is

\[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-4t}}=\sum_{n\ge1}\binom{2n-2}{n-1}t^{n-1}, \]

the multiplicities of the eigenvalue \(0\); and with the eigenvalue \(1\) included, \(s=1\) gives \(\sum_{n\ge1}\binom{2n}{n}t^{n-1}\), the numbers of states, as it must.

Proof. A word with \(a\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(b\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\) is counted in \(m_n(j)\) exactly when \(n-1\ge\max(a,b)\) and \(j=2(n-1)-a-b\). Writing \(q=n-1\), the left-hand side is therefore

\[ \sum_{a,b\ge0}\binom{a+b}{a}\sum_{q\ge\max(a,b)}s^{2q-a-b}t^q =\frac{1}{1-s^2t}\sum_{a,b\ge0}\binom{a+b}{a}s^{\lvert a-b\rvert}t^{\max(a,b)} . \]

The terms with \(a=b=q\) give \(\sum_q\binom{2q}{q}t^q=1/\sqrt{1-4t}\). The terms with \(\lvert a-b\rvert=d\ge1\) and \(\min(a,b)=q\) come in two orientations and give, by the identity \(\sum_{q\ge0}\binom{2q+d}{q}t^q=C(t)^d/\sqrt{1-4t}\),

\[ 2\sum_{d\ge1}(st)^d\frac{C(t)^d}{\sqrt{1-4t}} =\frac{2\,stC(t)}{\bigl(1-stC(t)\bigr)\sqrt{1-4t}} . \]

Adding the two contributions gives the formula. The value at \(s=1\) follows from \(\bigl(1-tC(t)\bigr)C(t)=1\) and \(\sqrt{1-4t}=1-2tC(t)\).

No closed form in the lower half

Definition 2 Let \(d\in\{1,2\}\). A function \(h\) on a set \(\mathcal D\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^2\) is a hypergeometric term (with step \(d\) in the second variable) if there are rational functions \(R_1,R_2\in\mathbb{Q}(x,y)\) such that \(h(n+1,j)=R_1(n,j)\,h(n,j)\) and \(h(n,j+d)=R_2(n,j)\,h(n,j)\) whenever both points lie in \(\mathcal D\).

Products and quotients of factorials of integer-linear forms in \(n\) and \(j\), multiplied by rational functions and powers, are hypergeometric terms; this is the usual meaning of a closed form for a single term (Petkovšek et al. 1996). Step \(d=2\) allows separate formulas for even and odd \(j\).

Theorem 3 Let \(\mathcal D=\{(n,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^2:n\ge2,\ 0\le j\le n-2\}\), the lower half of the spectrum, and for \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\) let \(\mathcal D_\varepsilon=\{(n,j)\in\mathcal D:j\equiv\varepsilon\pmod 2\}\). There is no hypergeometric term with step \(2\) on \(\mathcal D_\varepsilon\) that agrees with \(m_n(j)\) there. In particular, no hypergeometric term in \((n,j)\) agrees with \(m_n(j)\) on \(\mathcal D\), even if separate formulas are allowed for even and odd \(j\).

Proof. Change variables to \(p=n-1\) and \(L=2n-2-j\), so that \(j=2p-L\) and \(j\equiv L\pmod2\). The set \(\mathcal D_\varepsilon\) becomes \(\{(p,L):p\ge1,\ p+1\le L\le 2p,\ L\equiv\varepsilon\}\), and by Proposition 2(c) the multiplicity becomes

\[ M(p,L)=2B(L,p)-2^L,\qquad\text{so}\qquad M(p+1,L)-M(p,L)=2\binom{L}{p+1}. \]

Suppose \(h=M\) on \(\mathcal D_\varepsilon\) for a hypergeometric term \(h\) with step \(2\). The shift \((p,L)\mapsto(p+1,L)\) is \((n,j)\mapsto(n+1,j+2)\), so on \(W=\{(p,L):p+2\le L\le 2p,\ L\equiv\varepsilon\}\), where both points lie in the domain, \(h(p+1,L)=\rho(p,L)\,h(p,L)\) with \(\rho=R_1(n,j)R_2(n+1,j)\) rational. On \(W\) we have \(h(p,L)\bigl(\rho(p,L)-1\bigr)=2\binom{L}{p+1}>0\), so

\[ h(p,L)=2\,y(p,L)\binom{L}{p+1},\qquad y=\frac{1}{\rho-1}\in\mathbb{Q}(p,L). \]

Let \(W'=\{(p,L):p+3\le L\le 2p,\ L\equiv\varepsilon\}\), on which \((p,L)\) and \((p+1,L)\) both lie in \(W\). Substituting into \(M(p+1,L)-M(p,L)=2\binom{L}{p+1}\) and dividing by \(2\binom{L}{p+1}\ne0\) gives, on \(W'\),

\[ (L-p-1)\,y(p+1,L)-(p+2)\,y(p,L)=p+2 . \tag{6}\]

Clearing denominators turns Equation 6 into \(\Phi(p,L)=0\) for a polynomial \(\Phi\), valid at the points of \(W'\) off the zero set of some nonzero polynomial \(\Psi\). Then \(\Phi\Psi\) vanishes on \(W'\). For each large \(p\) the set \(W'\) contains about \(p/2\) values of \(L\), more than the degree of \(\Phi\Psi\) in \(L\), so every coefficient of \(\Phi\Psi\) as a polynomial in \(L\) vanishes at every large \(p\), and is therefore zero. Hence \(\Phi\Psi=0\), so \(\Phi=0\) and Equation 6 is an identity in \(\mathbb{Q}(p,L)\). Now regard \(L\) as transcendental over \(\mathbb{Q}\) and \(y\) as an element of \(K(p)\), \(K=\mathbb{Q}(L)\), and let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure of \(K\).

\(y\) has no poles in \(\overline K\). Suppose \(y\) has a pole \(\alpha\). The poles in \(\alpha+\mathbb{Z}\) form a finite nonempty set; let \(\alpha_+\) be its largest element and \(\alpha_-\) its smallest. At \(p=\alpha_+\) the function \(y(p+1)\) is regular, as is the right-hand side of Equation 6, so \((p+2)y(p)\) must be regular at \(\alpha_+\), which forces \(\alpha_+=-2\). At \(p=\alpha_--1\) the function \(y(p)\) is regular, so \((L-p-1)y(p+1)\) must be regular there, which forces \(L-\alpha_-=0\). Then \(\alpha_+-\alpha_-=-2-L\in\mathbb{Z}\), impossible since \(L\) is transcendental.

\(y\) is not a polynomial either. If \(y\ne0\) is a polynomial of degree \(e\) with leading coefficient \(c\), the left-hand side of Equation 6 has leading term \(-c\,p^{e+1}-c\,p^{e+1}=-2c\,p^{e+1}\), so \(e=0\) and \(y=c\) is constant; then \(c(L-2p-3)=p+2\) forces \(c=-\tfrac12\) and \(L=-1\), a contradiction. And \(y=0\) gives \(0=p+2\).

This contradiction proves the first statement. For the second, the restriction of a hypergeometric term with step \(1\) on \(\mathcal D\) to \(\mathcal D_\varepsilon\) is a hypergeometric term with step \(2\).

Remark. The argument is Gosper’s algorithm (R. W. Gosper 1978), (Petkovšek et al. 1996) (Ch. 5) applied to the partial row sums \(B(L,p)=\sum_{i\le p}\binom Li\) of Pascal’s triangle, the standard example of a sum with no closed form (in contrast with the alternating sums \(\sum_{i\le p}(-1)^i\binom Li=(-1)^p\binom{L-1}{p}\)). So the description of the multiplicities is genuinely piecewise: \(2^{2n-2-j}\) in the upper half \(j\ge n-1\), and in the lower half a partial row sum, for which the recurrence of Proposition 5 and the generating function of Proposition 6 are the simplest exact descriptions.

The chain in the group algebra

Let \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) act on words of any content by permuting positions, and on functions by \((\rho(\tau)f)(w)=f(w\circ\tau)\). By Equation 1,

\[ P=\frac1N\sum_{p=1}^N\rho(\pi_p), \]

the image of an element of the group algebra \(\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_N\). Hence \(P\) preserves every \(\mathfrak{S}_N\)-stable subspace, and the multiplicity of an eigenvalue of \(P\) on an irreducible submodule depends only on its isomorphism type. For \(a\ge b\) write \(M^{(a,b)}\) for the functions on words with \(a\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and \(b\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\); the same element of \(\mathbb{C}\mathfrak{S}_N\) acts on \(M^{(a,b)}\) as the chain for that deck. By Young’s rule (Sagan 2001),

\[ M^{(a,b)}\cong\bigoplus_{i=0}^{b}S^{(a+b-i,i)},\qquad \dim S^{(N-i,i)}=\binom Ni-\binom N{i-1}, \]

each Specht module occurring once. In particular \(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega_n}=M^{(n,n)}\cong S^{(2n)}\oplus S^{(2n-1,1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus S^{(n,n)}\), the top piece \(S^{(n,n)}\) has dimension \(C_n\), and \(M^{(2n-i,i)}\cong M^{(2n-i+1,i-1)}\oplus S^{(2n-i,i)}\).

Multiplicities on the pieces

Proposition 7 Let \(1\le i\le n\). On \(S^{(2n-i,i)}\) the eigenvalue \(j/N\) has multiplicity

\[ \binom{L}{i-1}-\binom{L}{2n-i}\quad(0\le j\le N-2,\ L=N-2-j), \]

and multiplicity \(0\) for \(j\in\{N-1,N\}\). The trivial module \(S^{(2n)}\) carries only the eigenvalue \(1\).

Proof. By the last isomorphism above, the multiplicity on \(S^{(2n-i,i)}\) is \(m_{(2n-i,i)}(j)-m_{(2n-i+1,i-1)}(j)\), the difference of the multiplicities for two decks. For \(j=N\) both are \(1\) and for \(j=N-1\) both are \(0\). Let \(j\le N-2\). For \(i\ge2\) both decks have two colours, and by Corollary 1 the two numbers count the words of length \(L\) with at most \(2n-i-1\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and at most \(i-1\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\), and with at most \(2n-i\) letters \(\mathtt{R}\) and at most \(i-2\) letters \(\mathtt{G}\). Counting by the number \(g\) of letters \(\mathtt{G}\), the first count runs over \(\max(0,L-2n+i+1)\le g\le i-1\) and the second over \(\max(0,L-2n+i)\le g\le i-2\), so the difference is \(\binom L{i-1}-\binom L{L-2n+i}=\binom L{i-1}-\binom L{2n-i}\). For \(i=1\) the second deck is the single word \(\mathtt{R}^{2n}\), with only the eigenvalue \(1\), and the first count is \(1=\binom L0-\binom L{2n-1}\). Finally \(M^{(2n,0)}=S^{(2n)}\) is the trivial module.

Corollary 2 Let \(0\le j\le N-2\) and \(L=N-2-j\).

(a) \(\sum_{i=1}^n\bigl[\binom L{i-1}-\binom L{2n-i}\bigr]=B(L,n-1)-\bigl(2^L-B(L,n-1)\bigr) =m_n(j)\), in agreement with Theorem 1.

(b) The standard representation \(S^{(N-1,1)}\) carries each of the eigenvalues \(0,1/N,\dots,(N-2)/N\) exactly once.

(c) On \(S^{(n,n)}\) the eigenvalue \(j/N\) has multiplicity

\[ \binom{L}{n-1}-\binom{L}{n}=f^{(n-1,\,n-1-j)}\quad(0\le j\le n-1), \]

and multiplicity \(0\) for \(j\ge n\). Here \(f^{\lambda}\) is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape \(\lambda\).

Proof. (a) uses \(L\le 2n-2\), so that \(\sum_{s=n}^{2n-1}\binom Ls=2^L-B(L,n-1)\). (b) is the case \(i=1\). (c) is the case \(i=n\), together with the hook length formula \(f^{(a,b)}=\binom{a+b}{b}-\binom{a+b}{b-1}\) for \(a=n-1\), \(b=n-1-j\), \(a+b=L\), and the symmetry of binomial coefficients. For \(j\ge n\) we have \(L\le n-2\), so both binomial coefficients vanish.

An explicit description of the pieces

Identify a word \(w\in\Omega_n\) with the set \(G(w)\subseteq[N]\) of positions of its letters \(\mathtt{G}\), an \(n\)-subset. For \(0\le i\le n\) let \(U_i\) send a function \(\phi\) on the \(i\)-subsets of \([N]\) to \((U_i\phi)(w)=\sum_{T\subseteq G(w),\,\lvert T\rvert=i}\phi(T)\). Each \(U_i\) is \(\mathfrak{S}_N\)-equivariant and injective (the inclusion matrices have full rank (Gottlieb 1966)), so \(\operatorname{Im}U_i\cong M^{(N-i,i)}\cong\bigoplus_{i'\le i}S^{(N-i',i')}\), and the \(S^{(N-i,i)}\)-component of \(\mathbb{C}^{\Omega_n}\) is the orthogonal complement of \(\operatorname{Im}U_{i-1}\) in \(\operatorname{Im}U_i\) (the standard inner product is invariant, and distinct isotypic components are orthogonal). For \(n\le6\) we computed these components, checked that \(P\) preserves each of them, and computed the spectrum of \(P\) on each; all agree with Proposition 7.

The colour swap

Lemma 3 Under the identification \(w\mapsto G(w)\), the colour swap \(\sigma\) sends an \(n\)-subset of positions to its complement. Thus \(S\) is the adjacency operator of the Kneser graph \(KG(2n,n)\), whose vertices are the \(n\)-subsets of \([2n]\), two being adjacent when they are disjoint. It commutes with \(\mathfrak{S}_N\) and acts on the component \(S^{(2n-i,i)}\) as the scalar \((-1)^i\).

Proof. Each \(n\)-subset of \([2n]\) is disjoint from exactly one other, its complement. The map commutes with permutations of positions, so by Schur’s lemma it acts on each (multiplicity-free) component by a scalar. The Kneser graph \(KG(N,n)\) has eigenvalue \((-1)^i\binom{N-n-i}{n-i}\) on the \(S^{(N-i,i)}\)-component (Lovász 1979; Godsil and Royle 2001), and for \(N=2n\) this is \((-1)^i\).

Theorem 4 The colour-symmetric and colour-antisymmetric parts of Section 2 are

\[ V_+=\bigoplus_{i\ \mathrm{even}}S^{(2n-i,i)},\qquad V_-=\bigoplus_{i\ \mathrm{odd}}S^{(2n-i,i)}. \]

For \(0\le j\le N-3\) the eigenvalue \(j/N\) has the same multiplicity, \(m_n(j)/2\), in \(V_+\) and in \(V_-\). The eigenvalue \((N-2)/N\) occurs only in \(V_-\) (once), and the eigenvalue \(1\) only in \(V_+\) (once). In particular \(m_n(j)\) is even for \(0\le j\le 2n-3\).

Proof. The description of \(V_\pm\) is Lemma 3. Let \(j\le N-2\). The trivial module carries only the eigenvalue \(1\), so by Proposition 7 the multiplicity of \(j/N\) in \(V_+\) minus that in \(V_-\) is

\[ \Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^i\Bigl[\binom{L}{i-1}-\binom{L}{2n-i}\Bigr]. \]

If \(L=0\), that is \(j=N-2\), only \(i=1\) contributes (as \(2n-i\ge n\ge1\)), so \((N-2)/N\) lies in \(V_-\) with multiplicity \(1\). If \(L\ge1\), use \(\sum_{r=0}^{q}(-1)^r\binom Lr=(-1)^q\binom{L-1}{q}\) and \(\sum_{r=0}^{L}(-1)^r\binom Lr=0\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^i\binom L{i-1}&=-(-1)^{n-1}\binom{L-1}{n-1},\\ \sum_{i=1}^n(-1)^i\binom L{2n-i}&=\sum_{s=n}^{2n-1}(-1)^s\binom Ls =-(-1)^{n-1}\binom{L-1}{n-1}, \end{aligned} \]

where the second computation uses \(L\le 2n-2\). Hence \(\Delta=0\). The eigenvalue \(1\) lies in the trivial module, which is in \(V_+\).

For example, for \(n=5\), Table 7 gives the following multiplicities of \(j/10\) for \(j=0,1,\dots,8\):

\[ V_+:\ 35,\,35,\,25,\,15,\,8,\,4,\,2,\,1,\,0,\qquad V_-:\ 35,\,35,\,25,\,15,\,8,\,4,\,2,\,1,\,1. \]

For \(n=2\) and \(n=3\) the theorem reproduces the splittings found in Section 3.

A proposed formula

It has been suggested that the multiplicity of the eigenvalue \(j/(2n)\) is

\[ s_n(j)=\binom{2n-j-1}{n-1}-\binom{2n-j-1}{n}, \tag{7}\]

first for all \(j\), and in a revised form only for \(1\le j\le 2n-2\). (In the original proposal the eigenvalue numerator was called \(k\).)

Proposition 8 Let \(s_n(j)\) be as in Equation 7.

(a) \(s_n(0)=0\), \(s_n(j)=f^{(n-1,\,n-j)}\) for \(1\le j\le n\), and \(s_n(j)=0\) for \(n<j\le 2n-1\). The values are ballot numbers, and \(\sum_{j=0}^{2n-1}s_n(j)=C_n\).

(b) For \(1\le j\le 2n-1\), \(s_n(j)\) is the multiplicity of the eigenvalue \((j-1)/(2n)\), one step below \(j/(2n)\), on the Specht module \(S^{(n,n)}\).

(c) As a formula for all \(j\), Equation 7 fails for every \(n\ge1\), since \(s_n(0)=0<m_n(0)\).

(d) For \(1\le j\le 2n-2\) we have \(s_n(j)\le m_n(j)\), with equality only for \((n,j)=(2,2)\). So the revised claim also fails for every \(n\ge2\) (for \(n=1\) its range is empty).

Proof. (b) By Corollary 2(c), the multiplicity of \((j-1)/N\) on \(S^{(n,n)}\) is \(\binom{L'}{n-1}-\binom{L'}{n}\) with \(L'=2n-2-(j-1)=2n-j-1\), which is \(s_n(j)\), for \(0\le j-1\le N-2\).

(a) The hook length formula gives \(f^{(n-1,n-j)}=\binom{2n-j-1}{n-j}-\binom{2n-j-1}{n-j-1}\), which equals \(s_n(j)\) by symmetry, for \(1\le j\le n\). For \(j>n\) the top index \(2n-j-1\) is less than \(n-1\), so \(s_n(j)=0\); and \(s_n(0)=\binom{2n-1}{n-1}-\binom{2n-1}{n}=0\). By (b) the sum is \(\dim S^{(n,n)}=C_n\).

(c) \(m_n(0)=\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\ge1\). For \(n=1\), for example, \(P\) has eigenvalues \(1\) and \(0\), while Equation 7 assigns multiplicity \(0\) to the eigenvalue \(0\) and multiplicity \(1\) to \(\tfrac12\).

(d) Compare with \(m_n(j)\), using Proposition 4 and \(L=2n-2-j\).

  • \(1\le j\le n-2\): here \(m_n(j)\) is a sum of consecutive binomial coefficients \(\binom Li\) including \(i=n-1\) and \(i=n-2\), so \(m_n(j)\ge\binom{L}{n-1}+\binom{L}{n-2}=\binom{2n-j-1}{n-1}\), while \(s_n(j)=\binom{2n-j-1}{n-1}-\binom{2n-j-1}{n}\) and \(\binom{2n-j-1}{n}\ge1\).
  • \(j=n-1\): \(s_n(j)=\binom n{n-1}-1=n-1<2^{n-1}=m_n(j)\).
  • \(j=n\): \(s_n(j)=1\le 2^{n-2}=m_n(j)\), with equality only for \(n=2\).
  • \(n+1\le j\le 2n-2\): \(s_n(j)=0<2^{2n-2-j}=m_n(j)\).

The smallest counterexample to the revised claim is \(n=2\), \(j=1\): Equation 7 gives \(\binom21-\binom22=1\), but the eigenvalue \(\tfrac14\) has the two independent eigenvectors \((1,-2,1,1,-2,1)\) and \((1,-2,1,-1,2,-1)\) of Table 2. In general, at \(j=1\) the proposed value is the Catalan number \(C_{n-1}\) while the true multiplicity is \(\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\). Table 8 and Table 9 show \(n=5\) and \(n=6\).

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""Checks for the note on the top-to-random chain on two-coloured words.

A  spectra for n = 1..6 against the multiplicity formula
B  spectra of other multiset decks against the general-content formula
C  exact eigenspace dimensions for n = 3 and 5 (integer row reduction)
D  traces of powers of 10P for n = 5, k = 0..18, in exact integer arithmetic
E  n = 2 eigenvectors and n = 3 sample rows quoted in the text
F  Specht pieces S^(2n-i,i) and the colour swap, n = 1..6
G  equivalent forms, Pascal's rule, small-j formulas (n <= 30),
   generating function (n <= 23)
H  the suggested formula C(2n-k-1, n-1) - C(2n-k-1, n)
Needs only numpy.  Takes about a minute, mostly for C and F.
"""
import itertools
from collections import Counter, defaultdict
from math import comb, gcd
import numpy as np

R, G = 0, 1


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- the chain
def chain(content):
    """States (tuples of colour indices) and the integer matrix N*P."""
    N = sum(content)
    base = [c for c, mult in enumerate(content) for _ in range(mult)]
    W = sorted(set(itertools.permutations(base)))
    ix = {w: a for a, w in enumerate(W)}
    M = np.zeros((len(W), len(W)), dtype=np.int64)
    for w in W:
        first, rest = w[0], w[1:]
        for s in range(N):                      # the N slots
            M[ix[w], ix[rest[:s] + (first,) + rest[s:]]] += 1
    return W, ix, M


def spectrum(M):
    """Eigenvalues of the integer matrix M (= N*P), rounded and counted."""
    ev = np.linalg.eigvals(M.astype(float))
    near = np.rint(ev.real).astype(int)
    assert np.abs(ev - near).max() < 1e-6
    return Counter(near.tolist()), np.abs(ev - near).max(), np.abs(ev.imag).max()


# ------------------------------------------------------------- the formulas
def m(n, j):
    """Multiplicity of j/(2n) for the chain on words with n R's and n G's."""
    N = 2 * n
    if j == N:
        return 1
    if j == N - 1:
        return 0
    L = N - 2 - j
    return sum(comb(L, i) for i in range(max(0, L - n + 1), min(L, n - 1) + 1))


def polymul(p, q):
    out = defaultdict(int)
    for a, x in p.items():
        for b, y in q.items():
            out[tuple(u + v for u, v in zip(a, b))] += x * y
    return out


def delta(deg, k):
    """delta_deg = sum_i (-1)^i e_i p_1^(deg-i) in k variables."""
    p1 = {tuple(int(t == i) for t in range(k)): 1 for i in range(k)}
    tot = defaultdict(int)
    for i in range(min(deg, k) + 1):
        e = {tuple(int(t in S) for t in range(k)): 1
             for S in itertools.combinations(range(k), i)}
        p = {(0,) * k: 1}
        for _ in range(deg - i):
            p = polymul(p, p1)
        for ex, c in polymul(e, p).items():
            tot[ex] += (-1) ** i * c
    return tot


def m_content(mu, j):
    """<h_j delta_{N-j}, h_mu>: multiplicity of j/N for a deck of content mu."""
    k, N = len(mu), sum(mu)
    d = delta(N - j, k)
    return sum(d.get(tuple(c - a for c, a in zip(mu, al)), 0)
               for al in itertools.product(*[range(c + 1) for c in mu])
               if sum(al) == j)


def piece(n, i, k):
    """Multiplicity of k/(2n) on the Specht module S^(2n-i,i)."""
    N = 2 * n
    if i == 0:
        return int(k == N)
    if k >= N - 1:
        return 0
    L = N - 2 - k
    return comb(L, i - 1) - comb(L, N - i)


def suggested(n, k):
    t = 2 * n - k - 1
    return comb(t, n - 1) - comb(t, n)

for n in range(1, 7):

    assert all(obs.get(j, 0) == m(n, j) for j in range(2 * n + 1))

            assert m_content(mu, j) == sum(comb(L, g) for g in range(L + 1)
                                           if g <= b - 1 and L - g <= a - 1)
    print("   content", mu, "ok:", pred)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------------ C
def rank_exact(A):
    """Rank over Q of an integer matrix, by integer row reduction."""
    rows = [[int(x) for x in r] for r in A]
    rank, ncol = 0, len(rows[0])
    for c in range(ncol):
        piv = next((i for i in range(rank, len(rows)) if rows[i][c]), None)
        if piv is None:
            continue
        rows[rank], rows[piv] = rows[piv], rows[rank]
        pr, a = rows[rank], rows[rank][c]
        for i in range(rank + 1, len(rows)):
            b = rows[i][c]
            if b:
                row = [a * x - b * y for x, y in zip(rows[i], pr)]
                g = 0
                for x in row:
                    g = gcd(g, x)
                rows[i] = [x // g for x in row] if g > 1 else row
        rank += 1
    return rank


print("C. eigenspace dimensions: exact (n = 3, 5), and by SVD (n = 5)")
for n in (3, 5):
    W, ix, M = chain((n, n))
    D = len(W)
    tot, zero_sv, nonzero_sv = 0, 0.0, np.inf
    for j in range(2 * n + 1):
        A = M - j * np.eye(D, dtype=np.int64)
        k1 = D - rank_exact(A)
        k2 = D - rank_exact(A @ A) if k1 else 0
        assert k1 == k2 == m(n, j)
        tot += k1
        sv = np.sort(np.linalg.svd(A.astype(float), compute_uv=False))
        if k1:
            zero_sv = max(zero_sv, sv[k1 - 1])
        nonzero_sv = min(nonzero_sv, sv[k1])
    assert tot == D
    print("   n=%d: dim ker(NP - jI) = dim ker(NP - jI)^2 = m(j) for all j; total %d"
          % (n, tot))
print("   n=5 SVD: largest 'zero' singular value %.1e, smallest nonzero %.3f"
      % (zero_sv, nonzero_sv))

def orth(A, tol=1e-9):
    U, s, _ = np.linalg.svd(A, full_matrices=False)
    return U[:, s > tol * s.max()]

def Dab(a, b):
    """Coloured derangement number D(a,b)."""
    if a == b == 0:
        return 1
    return 0 if a == 0 or b == 0 else comb(a + b - 2, a - 1)

def smul(A, B):

    return out
